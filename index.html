🧠 What Is yolo-mcp-vision?

yolo-mcp-vision is a small program that turns your Windows computer into a live object detection screen. You open it, point it at a camera or a video file, and it immediately shows what it sees — a green box appears around a person, a blue box around a car, and a label with a confidence score next to each one.

Behind the scenes it uses a popular vision engine called YOLO, which is famous for being fast enough to track moving objects as they happen. You never have to touch that engine yourself — it is already wired up and waiting inside the app.

The second ingredient is MCP, short for Model Context Protocol. In plain terms, it is a standard doorway that lets AI assistants peek at what your dashboard is seeing. If you already chat with an AI helper on your computer, that helper can ask "what is on screen right now?" and get a real answer, because the dashboard is broadcasting the results live.

✨ What You Can Do With It

  • Watch live object detection. The video panel updates many times every second, so boxes follow moving people and vehicles smoothly instead of jumping around.
  • Use your webcam or a video file. Show the app your built-in camera, a USB camera, or a recorded clip you already have on your PC.
  • Read a clear results list. Next to the video you get a tidy list of everything found, with counts and confidence levels, so you can see totals at a glance.
  • Connect an AI assistant. Turn on the MCP doorway and a compatible AI helper on your machine can ask questions about what the dashboard is currently detecting.
  • Keep a log of detections. The dashboard keeps the latest results visible so you can look back a few seconds and see what passed through the frame.
  • Stay offline. Detection happens on your own computer. Nothing is uploaded to a website, which keeps your camera feed private.

🖥️ What Your Computer Needs

Almost any modern Windows laptop or desktop will run this comfortably. Here is the practical checklist:

  • Windows 10 or Windows 11 (64-bit), fully updated.
  • 8 GB of memory or more. 4 GB may work, but 8 GB keeps the video smooth.
  • About 1 GB of free disk space for the program and its detection model.
  • A webcam if you want live camera detection. It is optional — video files work too.
  • An internet connection for the first launch so the app can complete its setup. After that it can run offline.

🚦 Your First Launch, Step by Step

  1. After the app opens, a web browser window appears with the dashboard inside it. This is normal — the dashboard talks to your browser and keeps everything tidy.
  2. You will see a large video area in the middle, a status row at the top, and a side panel listing detected objects.
  3. Press the start or play control, then allow camera access if Windows asks. Choose Allow.
  4. Hold a mug, a phone or a book up to the camera. Within a second, a coloured box should appear around it with a label.
  5. To stop, press the stop control. To close the app completely, close both the dashboard tab in your browser and the small black window that opened alongside it.

🔌 Letting an AI Assistant See the Results

This part is optional. If you never use an AI assistant, you can ignore it completely and the dashboard still works perfectly.

MCP is simply a shared language. When the dashboard is running, it offers a small set of questions an AI helper can ask, such as which objects are visible, how many there are, and when they were last seen. You enable this in the dashboard settings with a single toggle, then add the dashboard's address to whichever AI application you use.

The benefit is practical: instead of you describing the scene, the assistant can read the detection list itself and answer in plain language, like "there are two people and one car in view".

🛠️ Troubleshooting

The dashboard loads but the video stays black.

Another program is probably using the camera. Close video call applications, then reload the dashboard page and allow camera access again.

Nothing is being detected.

Detection works best in good light. Turn on a lamp, keep the subject within a metre or two of the camera, and give the dashboard a second or two to settle. Very small or very distant objects are harder to recognise.

The boxes appear but the labels are hard to read.

Move closer to the camera or make the browser window larger. The dashboard scales its labels with the video, so a bigger window gives you clearer text.

I do not see the MCP option.

Check that you are running the newest release from the download page. MCP support is part of the dashboard settings; if the option is missing, the version you have is older and should be replaced with the latest release.
